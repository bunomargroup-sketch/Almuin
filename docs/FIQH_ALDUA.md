# فقه الدعاء والذكر — guidance reference

**Source:** سلسلة مفاهيم — *فقه الدعاء والذكر*, سماحة الشيخ **محمد الحسن الددو
الشنقيطي**, interviewed by د. عادل بن أحمد باناعمة, edited with takhrīj by
د. علي بن حمزة العمري. First edition 1436H / 2015, transcribed from the Ramadan
programme *مفاهيم* on قنوات فور شباب.

**Status in this app:** a **secondary, advisory** source. It supplies fiqh and
adab guidance, and a list of items flagged as weak or non-prophetic. It is
**not** used as a source of dhikr or du'ā' text — see `DUA_SOURCING.md` for why.

Anything surfaced from this file must be labelled as the Shaykh's position and
attributed, and must never be styled or badged as scripture.

## Positions worth surfacing in-app

### On du'ā' itself
- **Du'ā' is worship before it is a request** — *"وحاصل ادعوا هو حاصل اعبدوا"*.
- **Insistence does not contradict certainty.** The supplicant is certain of one
  of three outcomes: reward, immediate fulfilment, or deferral and storing.
- **Du'ā' and qadar.** Two kinds of writing: أم الكتاب, which does not change,
  and the writing over the foetus (rizq, lifespan, deeds), which is conditional
  and does change. *"لا يردّ القدر إلا الدعاء"* operates through تزاحم الإرادتين.
- **Du'ā' after any act of worship is a locus of acceptance** (مظنّة إجابة),
  established by induction from the Qur'an and Sunnah.
- **It must be in a language the supplicant understands** — *"كلّ ما لا يفهمه
  الإنسان؛ ليس ذِكْرًا لله"*.

### The four species of الاعتداء في الدعاء
1. **Departing from transmitted wordings is *not* transgression.** ʿUrwah, Abū
   Bakr and Ibn ʿUmar all supplicated in their own words before the Prophet ﷺ
   without rebuke. The restriction applies only to fixed liturgical formulae
   (tashahhud and the like).
2. **Excessive loudness is** — *"إنكم لا تدعون أصم ولا غائبًا"*.
3. **Over-elaboration about the unseen is** (Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ: *"لقد اعتديت"*).
   Length alone is not a fault: *"لأن ما عند الله أطول وأكثر"*. Naming specific
   people or peoples is a confirmed sunnah (qunūt al-nāzilah).
4. **Contrived rhyming is** — natural sajʿ is not, and much transmitted du'ā'
   rhymes without strain.

### Practice
- **Order of post-prayer adhkār:** istighfār ×3 → *اللهم أنت السلام* → (Fajr: the
  tahlīl with يحيي ويميت) → tasbīḥ/taḥmīd/takbīr ×33 → tahlīl completing 100 →
  the composite tahlīl → آية الكرسي. Then du'ā' freely.
- **دُبُر كل صلاة** means **after** the salām.
- **Congregational du'ā' with the imām and āmīn is legislated**, since many
  people cannot supplicate well themselves. Absence of precedent is not proof:
  *"النفي أيضا حكم، ويحتاج إلى إثباته"*.
- **Raising the hands** is the default in du'ā'; on the minbar the imām raises
  one finger only, except in istisqāʾ.
- **Raising the hands in witr qunūt is not established from the Prophet ﷺ** — but
  the disagreement is خلاف سائغ and neither side is to be rebuked.
- **Wiping the face after du'ā':** the hadith is ḍaʿīf; فيه سعة, and no one who
  does it is to be rebuked.
- **Du'ā' at khatm al-Qur'an is legislated**; best placed in the witr qunūt.
  Elsewhere is خلاف الأولى but not bidʿah.
- **Du'ā' is permitted in every part of the prayer except rukūʿ and during
  al-Fātiḥah.**
- **Prayer beads and electronic counters are permitted** — a means takes the
  ruling of its end.
- **Measured cadence is permitted; performed melody is not.** The governing
  principle: du'ā' must not be recited the way the Qur'an is.

## Caveats on this source
It is a transcript of live speech: the Shaykh quoted from memory, and at least
one composite post-prayer dhikr in the book does not match the wording of the
collection it cites. The editor's gradings occasionally differ from the
Shaykh's own view in the body — he rejects Ibn Ḥajar's *ḥasan li-ghayrih* for
face-wiping, for instance. Where the two disagree, say so rather than
flattening it.
